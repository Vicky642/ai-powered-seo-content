## Post 1 — "We've Hit Peak Traffic" (2024)

> I'll say it plainly: 2024 was peak organic traffic for most sites.
>
> The data is clear:
> → AI Overviews are absorbing informational intent
> → Zero-click is accelerating, not slowing
> → Google is reducing outbound clicks to keep users in Search
>
> This doesn't mean SEO is dead. It means the metric has changed.
>
> The new metric: AI visibility.
>
> Not "did they click?" but "did they hear about us in an AI answer?"
>
> Brands that understand this shift are building mentions, citations, and entity authority.
> Brands still optimizing for clicks alone are playing last decade's game.

## Post 2 — "LLMs Don't Rank, They Recall" (2024)

> The biggest misconception about AI SEO:
>
> People think LLMs "rank" content like Google does.
>
> They don't. LLMs RECALL information from training data and retrieval sources.
>
> Implications:
>
> 1. Being mentioned in MANY places matters more than ranking #1 for one query
> 2. Reddit, YouTube, and Twitter/X are heavily weighted in LLM training
> 3. Recency matters — fresh content on trusted platforms gets indexed faster
> 4. Brand + topic association in context matters more than domain authority
>
> Optimize for recall, not ranking.
> Different game. Different playbook.

**Engagement:** ~5,900 likes | 445+ comments  
**Why this matters:** Fundamental reframing of how AI search works vs. traditional SEO

## Post 3 — "The YouTube + Reddit Strategy for LLM Visibility" (2025)

> If you want to appear in AI-generated answers, publish on YouTube and Reddit.
>
> Here's the data from my research:
>
> - YouTube is the #2 most-cited source in ChatGPT answers (after Wikipedia)
> - Reddit is in the top 5 for most consumer categories
> - Both platforms are heavily weighted in perplexity citations
>
> Practical playbook:
>
> → Record a YouTube video on your key topics (transcripts get indexed)
> → Participate authentically in Reddit communities (not spam)
> → Repurpose your best content as YouTube scripts
> → Let AI transcribe and reformat for other channels
>
> The brands investing in YouTube + Reddit now have a 12-18 month head start.

**Why this matters:** Data-backed multi-platform strategy with specific citation evidence

## Post 4 — "High-Fidelity Content Beats High-Volume Content" (2025)

> The AI content volume play is over.
>
> In 2022-2023: "Publish 100 AI articles and see what sticks."
> In 2025: That strategy actively hurts you.
>
> What wins now is high-fidelity content:
>
> → One comprehensive piece that answers ALL related questions
> → Built on original data or first-hand experience
> → Distributed across 5-10 relevant channels in native formats
> → Updated regularly as the topic evolves
>
> Shift from content calendar to content portfolio.
>
> A portfolio of 20 excellent pieces outperforms 200 average ones.
> Every. Single. Time.

**Engagement:** ~3,800 likes | 270+ comments  
**Why this matters:** Clear strategic positioning against the commoditized AI content volume play

## Post 5 — "The Retention Play Is Underrated" (2025)

> Everyone obsesses over AI SEO acquisition.
>
> Nobody talks about AI SEO retention.
>
> As organic traffic decreases from AI Overviews, the traffic you DO get is higher intent.
>
> These visitors:
> → Already heard about you from an AI answer
> → Came to verify or go deeper
> → Are closer to a purchase decision
>
> The smart move: obsess over what happens AFTER the click.
>
> → Email capture with genuine value
> → Content upgrade paths
> → Internal linking to purchase-adjacent content
>
> The AI era doesn't mean less valuable traffic. It means more concentrated, higher-intent traffic.
> Are you built to convert it?
