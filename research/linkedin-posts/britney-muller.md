## Post 2 — "RAG Changes How You Should Think About Content" (2024)

> Most AI answers are powered by RAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
>
> Here's what this means for your content:
>
> RAG systems retrieve relevant chunks of content, then generate an answer using those chunks.
>
> Implications:
>
> 1. **Your content needs to be "chunkable"** — clear sections that can be retrieved independently
> 2. **Headers matter more than ever** — they define chunk boundaries
> 3. **Each section should stand alone** — don't assume the reader has the context from earlier
> 4. **Dense, information-rich paragraphs win** — low-density content gets skipped
>
> Write content that's easy to retrieve in pieces.
>
> Not one long narrative. A collection of high-value, independently useful sections.

**Engagement:** ~5,800 likes | 470+ comments  
**Why this matters:** RAG architecture explained in practical content production terms — rarely this clear elsewhere

## Post 4 — "Why AI Content Feels Generic (And How to Fix It)" (2025)

> AI content feels generic because LLMs average their training data.
>
> They produce the statistical center of what's been written about a topic.
> That center is, by definition, mediocre.
>
> How to push past generic:
>
> → **Add friction**: challenge a common assumption in your industry
> → **Add data**: specific numbers make content concrete and citable
> → **Add experience**: what happened when YOU applied this? Different from theory.
> → **Add stakes**: why does this matter? What's the cost of getting it wrong?
> → **Add personality**: your specific voice can't be averaged
>
> AI writes the median. Expert editors push it to the exceptional.
>
> That's the human's role in AI content production: quality control and differentiation.
