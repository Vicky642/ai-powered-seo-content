## Post 1 — "How Ahrefs Produces AI Content That Actually Ranks" (2025)

> We've been using AI for content at Ahrefs for 18+ months.
>
> Here's the system that works:
>
> **Step 1: Deep research brief (human)**
> - Real keyword data from Ahrefs
> - SERP analysis: what's ranking and why
> - Angle differentiation: what's MISSING from existing results
>
> **Step 2: Foundational instruction file (human → reused)**
> - Brand voice guidelines
> - Structural rules
> - Quality standards
>
> **Step 3: AI-assisted outline (AI + human review)**
> - AI generates; human validates against SERP
>
> **Step 4: Full draft (AI)**
> - Writes to the outline using the instruction file
>
> **Step 5: Editorial review (human)**
> - Add data, examples, opinions
> - Cut AI filler
> - Verify all facts
>
> **Step 6: Publish and monitor**
>
> Steps 1, 2, and 5 are where we invest. Everything else AI handles.
> That's what makes it scalable AND good.

## Post 2 — "What Content Gets Cited By AI? (With Data)" (2025)

> We analyzed 10,000 AI Overview citations across 50 content categories.
>
> What gets cited most:
>
> ✅ Content with original data (2.8x more likely to be cited)
> ✅ Content from recognized author entities (2.1x)
> ✅ Long-form comprehensive pages (1.9x vs. thin content)
> ✅ Content with FAQ sections (1.7x)
> ✅ Content on established domains (1.6x — DR matters)
>
> What gets cited least:
> ❌ Thin listicles under 800 words
> ❌ Content without clear authorship
> ❌ Outdated content (3+ years without updates)
> ❌ Content buried in JavaScript (AI can't render it)
>
> The playbook writes itself from this data.

## Post 3 — "The Foundational Instruction File Changes Everything" (2025)

> The single most underrated AI writing technique:
>
> The Foundational Instruction File.
>
> It's a document you create ONCE that tells ChatGPT (or any LLM):
> → Your brand voice
> → Your audience
> → Your quality standards
> → Examples of ideal content
> → Things to avoid
>
> Without it: every AI request starts from zero. Inconsistent, generic output.
> With it: every AI request builds on your standards. Consistent, on-brand output.
>
> Ours is ~2,000 words. We update it quarterly.
> It's probably the highest-leverage document in our content operation.

## Post 4 — "Winning In AI Search: What The Data Actually Shows" (2025)

> After months of tracking AI Overview and ChatGPT citation data:
>
> Here's what actually wins in AI search:
>
> 1. **Be the study.** Original research gets exponentially more citations.
> 2. **Be the definition.** Clear, authoritative definitions of industry terms.
> 3. **Be the comparison.** "X vs Y" content is heavily featured in AI answers.
> 4. **Be the process.** Step-by-step tutorials get cited across LLMs.
> 5. **Be the number.** Statistics and data points anchor AI-generated answers.
>
> Notice what's not here: keyword density, word count, link count.
>
> AI search doesn't care about traditional SEO signals as much as it cares about clarity, authority, and usefulness.

**Engagement:** ~7,300 likes | 580+ comments  
**Why this matters:** Data-driven framework that reframes AI SEO around content type rather than traditional signals

## Post 5 — "Stop Worrying About AI Detecting Your AI Content" (2025)

> The AI detection debate is a distraction.
>
> I've seen:
> → Perfectly human content flagged as AI-written
> → Excellent AI-assisted content ranking #1 for years
>
> Google's stated position: we evaluate content on quality and usefulness. Not on how it was produced.
>
> The real question isn't "will this get detected?"
>
> The real question is: "does this help the person searching?"
>
> If yes → publish it.
> If no → don't. Regardless of whether AI wrote it.
>
> Stop running your content through AI detectors.
> Start running it through a "would this actually help someone?" test.

**Engagement:** ~4,100 likes | 320+ comments  
**Why this matters:** Cuts through the AI detection anxiety with a clear quality-first principle
